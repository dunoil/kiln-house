Eight classes, one kiln room, no two pots alike. KILN HOUSE is a small pottery studio that teaches one term at a time: every class is capped at six students, every piece is fired in the studio kilns, and every glaze on the shelf is mixed here and tested on the same clay your hands are learning. This term has 24 students across eight classes, three classes already full, and five firings booked between 4 and 13 December. The glaze shelf (8)

Every glaze is mixed in the studio and tested on the same clay your hands are learning. One card per glaze, in the batch.

Celadon (KH-GLZ-01) · Cone 6 · Gloss, translucent jade · The classic green that pools in the throwing rings. Best on porcelain-white stoneware, thin where the clay shows through.

Shino (KH-GLZ-02) · Cone 6 · Matte, warm cream with orange flashing · A warm cream that flashes orange where the flame touches it. Hand-built work takes it best, thick on the rim.

Tenmoku (KH-GLZ-03) · Cone 6 · Gloss, deep iron brown-black · Iron brown so dark it reads black until the light catches it. The glaze of the glazing class, and of every good teabowl.

Ash Grey (KH-GLZ-04) · Cone 6 · Matte, soft wood-ash grey · Mixed from the studio's own wood ash. Quiet, grey, a little different on every pot, which is exactly why the large-form class uses it.

Copper Red (KH-GLZ-05) · Cone 06 · Metallic red, raku only · A raku glaze that comes out of the smoke bin a different red every time. Never in the electric kilns, never twice the same.

Cobalt Blue (KH-GLZ-06) · Cone 6 · Gloss, deep cobalt blue · A strong blue that holds its depth on pouring vessels. One dip, no second coat, or it runs off the spout.

Oatmeal (KH-GLZ-07) · Cone 6 · Matte, speckled oatmeal · Speckled and soft, the family class glaze: forgiving, food safe, and it hides a wobbly rim better than any other.

Iron Brown (KH-GLZ-08) · Cone 6 · Satin, warm iron brown · Warm iron brown with a satin skin. Sculptural work wears it well, it follows every fingerprint instead of hiding it.
